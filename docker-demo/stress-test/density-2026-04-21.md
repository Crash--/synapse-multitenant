# Density test results — 2026-04-21

Two runs at 10 and 50 tenants, same 200-VU k6 profile (`stress-test-noroom.js`), same ramp. Purpose: validate whether the F#1/F#2/F#3 fixes hold as tenant count scales and characterize where the architecture starts to show seams.

## Environment

- Branch: `feature/multi-tenant` @ `70437a392` (parametrized harness, `cp_max=50`, metrics enabled).
- `SYNAPSE_MT_STRICT_STATE_GROUPS=1` on throughout both runs.
- Host: 15 GB RAM laptop, same machine as the 4-tenant run.
- Stack: clean `docker compose down -v && up -d` before each run.

**Isolation holds under density.** **Fairness starts to show seams around 50 tenants** — not a correctness failure, but a signal that the design's load distribution stops being uniform once VUs-per-tenant drops below double digits.

## Memory — what does a tenant cost?

| Phase | 4 tenants | 10 tenants | 50 tenants |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 — cold boot, 0 tenants registered | ~146 MB | 146 MB | 147 MB |
| T1 — N tenants + N×10 users, idle | ~148 MB | 150 MB | 158 MB |
| Peak under 200-VU load | ~171 MB | 171 MB | 186 MB |

**Per-tenant baseline cost (idle):**

- 4 → 10 tenants: +4 MB over 6 tenants → **0.67 MB / tenant**
- 10 → 50 tenants: +8 MB over 40 tenants → **0.20 MB / tenant**

Sub-linear. The marginal cost of a new tenant drops as the baseline amortizes. This is the opposite of the principal assessment's pessimistic guess — at least at this density, per-tenant footprint SHRINKS with tenant count.

**Per-tenant working-set cost (active load):**

- 4 tenants: ~7 MB/tenant (171-146 = 25 MB active / 4)
- 10 tenants: ~2.5 MB/tenant (171-146 = 25 MB / 10)
- 50 tenants: ~0.8 MB/tenant (186-147 = 39 MB / 50)

Also sub-linear. Explanation: the 200 VUs spread over 50 tenants give each tenant only ~4 concurrent VUs, so most caches per tenant stay small.

**Projection:** extrapolating the sub-linear trend, 500 tenants would fit in ~300-400 MB RSS total. The RAM ceiling on a modern laptop is clearly not the limit — the meaningfulness of a 200-VU test with 0.4 VU/tenant is.

## Performance — what does a tenant cost in latency?

| Metric | 4 tenants | 10 tenants | 50 tenants |
|---|---|---|---|
| `versions` p95 | 3.1 ms | 3.7 ms | 18.8 ms |
| `whoami` p95 | 2.9 ms | 5.4 ms | 8.9 ms |
| `sync` p95 | 6.5 ms | 11.4 ms | 21.0 ms |
| `login` p95 | 238 ms | 296 ms | 403 ms |
| **`http_req_duration` p95** | **4.89 ms** | **8.83 ms** | **17.6 ms** |

Growth is sub-linear in tenant count (5× tenants → 2× p95). Still inside the 5000-ms threshold by three orders of magnitude. Login climbed more — likely the bcrypt CPU contention tightening as `cp_max` fills.

At 50 tenants the `versions` endpoint starts looking unusual (18.8 ms vs 3.7 ms at 10). `versions` is a static unauth'd endpoint — the tenant-lookup (hostname → TenantConfig) is the dominant cost, and at 50 entries the registry's `O(N)` linear scan (or rather: dict of 50 items with hostname aliases) starts showing up.

**Top 5 fastest:**

| Tenant | Mean | Max |
|---|---|---|
| `stress-005` | 2.63 ms | 96 ms |
| `stress-021` | 2.68 ms | 73 ms |
| `stress-039` | 2.78 ms | 66 ms |

| `stress-023` | 2.86 ms | 66 ms |

`stress-050` being the slowest is suspicious — it is the LAST provisioned tenant and carries the highest number in any registry linear scan. Worth investigating: tenant-lookup cost may be order-dependent (later entries pay more, perhaps because hostname aliases are checked in provision order), or its caches were coldest when the ramp hit its peak.

**Caveat on the spread number:** each 50-tenant sample has only 245–429 iterations (vs 1,549+ at 10 tenants). Low sample count inflates variance. Part of the 3.62× spread is statistics, not architecture. A longer run at 50 tenants would narrow the number; a 4-hour run is the clean way to separate noise from real inequity.

## Operational notes

- **Provisioning time for 50 tenants: 199 seconds** (~4 s per tenant, dominated by schema-clone). Sub-linear: at 10 tenants we saw ~3 min per previous runs, at 50 we're ~3.3 min — the clone itself parallelizes poorly but there's less per-tenant overhead.
- **Tenant-registry reloads per setup: 51** — one per provisioning call, plus initial startup. At 500 tenants that's 500 `/reload` calls, each iterating the registry and rebuilding keyring/ratelimiters. Consider batch-reload in the control plane.
- **Host impact:** Synapse peaked at 186 MB RSS and 136% of one core briefly. Rest of the stack negligible. Laptop stayed fully responsive throughout all runs.

## What to measure next

1. **100 tenants, same 200-VU profile.** Confirms whether p95 scaling stays sub-linear and whether the `stress-050`-style last-tenant penalty is order-related or statistical.
2. **Long-haul at 50 tenants (4 hours at 50 VUs).** Memory growth over time, cache eviction behavior, and whether the fairness spread tightens with larger samples.
3. **Tenant-registry O(N) characterization.** The `versions` endpoint jump from 3.7 ms (10 tenants) to 18.8 ms (50 tenants) is the clearest "something is linear in N" signal. Profile hostname→tenant resolution.
4. **`load_tenants_from_database` at 500+ tenants, idle.** Measures startup hydration cost without load. Tells us what operational cold-start looks like.

## What these runs do NOT prove

Unchanged from the 2026-04-21 assessment: no evidence for long-haul stability, tenant size asymmetry, federation at density, hot-add, or workers. These runs are a scaling smoke test — they show the mechanism holds at 50 tenants; they do not show SaaS-readiness.
